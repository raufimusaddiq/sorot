# Session 2026-10-28 — runs on 4 vCPU or fewer (perf/low-cpu)

Audit of everything besides the render (PR #17 did the render). Measured on a
4-core container with a synthetic 1-hour 1080p30 H.264 file (High profile,
5 s GOP, ~4 Mbit/s); Whisper itself not measured (huggingface.co blocked there).

## Found
- **Scoring lost its visual signals on long videos.** `features/visual.py`
  decoded every frame of the source to sample 1 fps, inside an ffmpeg call
  with a fixed 120 s limit. 1-hour video: hit the limit, all 86 segments
  scored "no face", default scene/motion — silently. Full decode: 264 s.
- Two pipelines at once by default, CPU too → two Whisper runs, each on every
  core and with its own model in RAM.
- Every PRIMARY/BACKUP clip rendered (README's own example: 45 for an hour).
- Retry re-ran Whisper although the transcript was on disk.
- CPU transcription limit 1.5x the media length: README's 12-core figure is
  ~0.5x, so 4 cores sit near the limit.
- Audio features held the whole WAV as float64 (+ raw bytes): ~1 GB per hour.

## Changed
- Frames at the nearest **keyframe** (`-skip_frame nokey`, times from
  `showinfo`); face majority over distinct frames (`_face_need`); sparse
  keyframes (>10% of samples further than 3 s) → 1 fps full decode with a
  limit that grows with the span. The 60-timestamp cap is gone (it suited one
  ffmpeg per time).
- CPU vs GPU from `WHISPER_DEVICE`: `MAX_CONCURRENT_PIPELINES` 1 / 2,
  `MAX_RENDER_CLIPS` 10 / all. Clips past the cap stay PENDING; studio
  **Render** button = `POST /rerender {}`. Editor renders now queue behind
  `maxParallelRenders` (several Render clicks don't all encode at once).
- TRANSCRIBE reuses a transcript whose `model`/`language` match.
- CPU transcription limit 4x media length.
- float32 audio, squares summed in float64, raw bytes freed early.
- reframe cut scan: `scale=160:-2:flags=fast_bilinear` (same cut times on a
  hard-cut test clip).
- `WHISPER_BEAM_SIZE` env (default 5 = unchanged).
- Dropped from the audit list: LLM batch timeouts (only bites a local LLM on
  the same CPU; the user uses a hosted API).

## Measured (master → branch, same machine, nothing else running)
- media_features.py on the hour: 120.9 s (limit hit, 86/86 segments lost
  visual signals), peak 1035 MB → 32.2 s, 0/86 lost, peak 485 MB.
- Face test (astronaut close-up on/off, GOP 2 s / 5 s / 60 s, 10 segments
  each): face and scene scores identical to the old 1 fps sampling; motion one
  band off in 4 of 30; the 60 s GOP took the full-decode fallback.
- Render, 4 x 40 s clips, face framing, 2 at a time: 24.1 → 22.2 s per clip.
  2 at a time vs 1: 69 → 56 s (centre), 117 → 92 s (face) — kept at 2.
- With 45 renderable clips: ~17 min of rendering at 22 s each; the CPU cap of
  10 makes it ~4 min.

## Follow-ups in the same PR
- **Range imports:** `--force-keyframes-at-cuts` makes yt-dlp drop `-c copy`
  (read in yt-dlp's `external.py`), so the section was re-encoded at ffmpeg's
  defaults. Real yt-dlp against a local range-capable HTTP server, 60 s of a
  5 s-GOP 1080p file: 45.7 s forced vs 1.3 s copy. ffmpeg reads the copy on
  the source timeline (frames at 0.5 / 5 / 30 s match the source within one
  frame, bit-identical — the re-encode was 55-57 dB). Now copy unless YouTube
  captions are on (not checked on YouTube's separate video/audio streams).
- **Transcription progress:** `transcribe.py` prints `PROGRESS n` (segment end
  ÷ `info.duration`, ≤ 99) on stderr; `spawnCapture` streams stderr lines
  (`onStderrLine`), `createPythonRunner` turns them into `onProgress` and strips
  them from logs/errors; TRANSCRIBE's `outputPath` reads "Transcribing n%";
  the UI's ProgressBar shows live text for TRANSCRIBE / RENDER / SUBTITLE.

## Real-machine test pass (Windows 11, RTX 4060, Docker Desktop)
- GPU (Ubuntu 24.04 cuda image): 11.5 min range → TRANSCRIBE 45 s, every
  PRIMARY/BACKUP clip rendered with `h264_nvenc`, no libx264 fallback lines;
  "Transcribing n%" moves. Jev gate live (jev-eval 12/12, rules 9/12).
- CPU, 4 cores (`cpus: 4` + `cpuset: "0-3"`; `cpus` alone leaves nproc = 12
  and Whisper runs 12 threads): 30 min range → TRANSCRIBE 16 min 33 s
  (~0.55x real time) at beam 5, 11 min 28 s at `WHISPER_BEAM_SIZE=1` (-31%,
  17% word diff vs beam 5, mostly punctuation/splits, neither clearly worse).
  10 clips auto-rendered (exactly 10 were ≥ 0.65, so the cap didn't bite);
  Render on a SKIP clip: 13 s. Face 0-1, scene 0.4-1: sensible.
- `install.ps1 -Cpu` without `-Build` pulls the published (older) image.
- Range import was slow: `--download-sections` goes through ffmpeg, one long
  request YouTube throttles to ~1.5x real time (60 s section: 40 s), while
  yt-dlp's own downloader does ~18x (63 min, 1.35 GB: 212 s). A JS runtime /
  EJS solver didn't change it; ffmpeg 6.1 has no chunked-request option.
- Fix (fix/range-import-speed): the title `--print` also prints duration; a
  range ≥ 10% of the video downloads whole to `raw/{id}.whole.mp4`, is cut
  locally (copy, or libx264 exact with YouTube captions) and the whole file
  removed. 13:25-25:00 import: 356 s → 199 s, cut 695.8 s, job COMPLETED.
  Backend 345/345, orchestrator.ts 100%, tsc clean.
